I have a weakness for new software. Every few months something arrives claiming to be the last tool I will ever need, and for about nine days it is.

This list is different: these are the things that were still open in December.

## The keepers

**A plain text file for everything.** Notes, drafts, half-ideas, phone numbers. Plain text has outlived every app I have ever loved, including two that no longer exist. It opens instantly and I can search it with one keystroke.

**A single calendar with nothing on it I dread.** If an item makes me sigh every time I see it, it is either delegated, deleted, or honestly scheduled. The calendar is not a wish list.

**A task list that holds five things.** Not fifty. Five. If a sixth arrives, something has to leave. The constraint is the feature.

## The three questions I ask before installing anything

1. What does this replace?
2. What will I stop doing because of it?
3. Would I still use it if nobody could see that I did?

Question three is the honest one. Half of my past tool-hopping was buying an identity rather than a capability.

> The best tool is the one you stop noticing.

## What I let go of

Dashboards I opened weekly to admire. Three apps that were one spreadsheet in a costume. And a beautifully designed writing app with no export — anything that cannot leave is not a tool, it is a landlord.

Nothing here is exotic. That is rather the point.
